### H2: The Destination Wedding Chef's Responsibilities

**Word Count:** 500 words

**Pre-Wedding Planning**
- Menu consultation (understanding couple's vision, cultural preferences, guest backgrounds)
- Dietary survey collection (allergies, vegetarian/vegan, religious, medical diets)
- Sourcing strategy (Bali local ingredients, international imports if needed)
- Team coordination (kitchen design, assistant chefs, serving staff)
- Timeline creation (rehearsal dinner through reception)

**Why This Matters:**
- Destination weddings have multiple events (welcome dinner, rehearsal dinner, reception, brunch)
- Guest demographics vary (international guests with different food cultures)
- Kitchen setup is often unfamiliar (villas, resorts with different equipment)
- Dietary needs are serious (allergies can be life-threatening at weddings)

**Wedding Week Coordination**
- Arrivals coordination (chef meets couple, understands kitchen layout)
- Ingredient delivery timeline (when specialty items arrive, storage setup)
- Staff briefing (how service will flow, pacing between courses)
- Logistics planning (timing, plating areas, serving stations)

**Example:** A 75-person wedding with 12 dietary restrictions across 4 countries. Chef creates master spreadsheet, sources specialty ingredients (gluten-free for 3 guests, halal-certified for 2, vegan proteins for 8), plans 5-course menu that celebrates Balinese cuisine while accommodating restrictions.

### H2: Wedding Menu Design: Creating the Perfect Progression

**Word Count:** 600 words

**Understanding Wedding Menu Strategy**

**Welcome Reception**
- Light appetizers (bites, not full meals)
- Signature cocktail or welcome drink
- Purpose: Guests arrive, mingle, set tone

**Example Menu:**
- Bali satay skewers with peanut sauce
- Crispy spring rolls with sweet chili
- Balinese bruschetta with roasted tomato

**Rehearsal Dinner** (if included)
- Casual but elegant
- Single main + sides
- Opportunity to test kitchen logistics

**Reception: Multi-Course Progression**

**Course 1: Amuse-Bouche** (1 small bite)
- Flavor: Bright, unexpected
- Purpose: Excites palate, signals "this will be special"
- Example: Coconut foam with passion fruit caviar

**Course 2: Appetizer** (if formal; skip for casual)
- Temperature: Usually cold or light
- Flavor: Complements main, doesn't overshadow
- Example: Bali seafood ceviche with coconut milk

**Course 3: Main Course**
- Options: Multiple mains for vegetarian/omnivore split
- Protein focus: Highlight Bali sourcing (local fish, beef, chicken)
- Sides: Vegetables, starch (rice, potatoes)
- Example:
  - Omnivore: Bali snapper with turmeric butter, jasmine rice, sautéed spinach
  - Vegetarian: Grilled heirloom tomato stack with coconut cream, wild rice, charred broccolini

**Course 4: Palate Cleanser** (optional, formal weddings)
- Temperature: Cold or sorbet
- Flavor: Refreshing, light acidity
- Example: Passion fruit sorbet with mint

**Course 5: Dessert**
- Main dessert: Couple's signature or chef's signature
- Often reflects theme (tropical for Bali, cultural for couple's background)
- Example: Coconut panna cotta with fresh mango, coconut tuile

**Course 6: Coffee/Tea Service**
- After dinner ritual
- Optional petit fours or macarons

### H2: Managing Dietary Restrictions at Weddings

**Word Count:** 400 words

**The Challenge**
- Average wedding has 10-20% of guests with dietary needs
- Allergies are serious (anaphylaxis risk)
- Medical diets are non-negotiable (heart health, diabetes, kidney disease)
- Religious or ethical diets must be respected

**Pre-Wedding Survey Process**
1. Couple creates dietary form (Google Form, Typeform)
2. Guest list provided to form
3. Each guest indicates: allergies, vegetarian/vegan, religious, medical, other preferences
4. Chef reviews compiled data (creates master spreadsheet)
5. Chef identifies clusters (e.g., 8 vegetarians, 3 celiac, 5 gluten-free-"just prefer it")
6. Menu adapted or separate dishes created

**Allergy Management**
- Severity levels: Anaphylaxis (EpiPen risk) vs. digestive discomfort
- Cross-contamination prevention: Separate prep surfaces, utensils, storage
- Communication: Clear labeling, verbal confirmation before serving
- Emergency prep: Know if anyone has EpiPen, locate nearest hospital

**Example:** Destination wedding with 12 guests having celiac or gluten sensitivity. Chef:
- Sources certified gluten-free ingredients
- Uses dedicated cutting board, knives, prep space
- Verifies all sauces, broths, seasonings are GF
- Creates separate plating station (no cross-contamination)
- Labels each dish clearly at service

**Vegetarian/Vegan Coordination**
- Not an afterthought "salad"
- Equal effort and creativity as omnivore mains
- Protein focus: Legumes, nuts, tofu, seitan, creative combinations

**Medical Diet Adaptation**
- Low-sodium for heart health: Remove added salt, use herbs/spices
- Diabetic-friendly: Monitor carbs, emphasize vegetables, use sugar alternatives
- Kidney disease: Control potassium/phosphorus (limits bananas, nuts, dairy)
- Adaptation doesn't mean "less flavorful" — it means creative technique

### H2: Budget & Cost Breakdown for Wedding Catering

**Word Count:** 350 words

**Cost Variables**

**Per-Person Catering Cost**
- Budget option: $40-60/person (casual, limited courses)
- Mid-range: $60-90/person (3-4 courses, quality ingredients, customized)
- Fine dining: $90-150+/person (5-6 courses, premium ingredients, Michelin-trained)

**Example: 75-Person Wedding**

**Mid-Range ($60/person):**
- Food cost: $30-35/person ($2,250-2,625)
- Chef labor: $15-20/person ($1,125-1,500)
- Service staff: $8-10/person ($600-750)
- Equipment/setup: $5-7/person ($375-525)
- Total: $4,350-5,400

**Fine Dining ($100/person):**
- Food cost: $50-60/person ($3,750-4,500)
- Chef labor: $25-30/person ($1,875-2,250)
- Service staff: $12-15/person ($900-1,125)
- Equipment/premium service: $10-15/person ($750-1,125)
- Total: $7,275-9,000

**Hidden Costs to Budget For**
- Dietary-specific ingredients (GF specialty items, vegan proteins): +5-10%
- International ingredients (imported from Europe/Asia): +15-25%
- Specialty equipment rental (extra ovens, warming tables): +$500-1,500
- Staff accommodation (if bringing team): +$100-300/person
- Day-of modifications: Buffer 10% of budget

**Money-Saving Tips**
- Embrace Bali seasonal ingredients (cheaper, fresher)
- Limit dietary accommodations (or charge upcharge for complex needs)
- Single entrée (vs. multiple options) reduces cost
- Daytime reception (brunch) usually cheaper than evening
- All-inclusive resort venues may include kitchen facilities (cheaper)

### H2: Frequently Asked Questions

**Word Count:** 300 words

**Q: How far in advance should I book a wedding chef?**
A: 3-6 months for fine dining chefs, 6-8 weeks for mid-range. Allow time for menu consultations and sourcing specialty items.

**Q: Can a private chef handle 100+ guests?**
A: Typically 50-75 is the sweet spot for one chef with assistants. 100+ guests require catering company or chef team.

**Q: What if guests don't RSVP with dietary info?**
A: Create a "default safe" menu (vegetarian option, no common allergens, basic preparation). Have backup options at reception.

**Q: Can the couple do a tasting before the wedding?**
A: Yes, highly recommended. Schedule 2-3 months before wedding (chef prepares mini versions of planned menu).

**Q: What if the wedding kitchen is really limited?**
A: Skilled private chef adapts. Plan menu around available equipment. Bring portable equipment if needed. Discuss constraints early.

**Q: Is the cost per person worth it vs. restaurant catering?**
A: Often yes. Private chefs offer: customization, reliability, flexibility, and superior quality. Restaurants offer: volume at lower cost. For intimate weddings, private chef wins.

**Q: Can a private chef handle international guests with different preferences?**
A: Absolutely. That's their specialty. Survey guests, identify patterns, create diverse menu that respects cultures.

**Q: What if allergies change last-minute?**
A: Alert chef immediately. Most can adapt quickly (particularly shellfish allergies, which are managed by sourcing).
