# 16. Scoring System & Scorecard

## 16.1 Scoring Methodology

### 16.1.1 Scale, Dimensions, and Weighting

Every page across myCHEF.id was scored on a 1-to-10 scale across six dimensions drawn from the five specialist audits conducted during this review. The dimensions are: **UX (Simplicity & Navigation)**, **Visual Design**, **Copywriting**, **SEO**, **Brand/Positioning**, and **Trust**. Each dimension receives an integer or half-point score based on evidence collected by the relevant specialist auditor. The page total is the unweighted arithmetic mean of all scored dimensions — no dimension is weighted higher than another because a premium hospitality brand cannot afford a single weak link. A score of 10 represents best-in-class execution; 5 represents functional but flawed; 1 represents critical failure.

The Staffing page (/staffing) is treated as a special case. It currently redirects to /contact with no unique content, design, or copy. Dimensions where no content exists receive "N/A" and are excluded from the mean, producing a composite of 1.1/10 — the only score below 2.0 on the entire site.

The scoring system is intentionally ruthless. A hospitality brand charging IDR 2,200,000++ per guest for fine dining does not get partial credit for "good enough." Every half-point deduction represents a specific, identifiable failure documented in the preceding 15 chapters. The scores are not aspirational; they are diagnostic.

| Score Range | Grade | Interpretation |
|-------------|-------|----------------|
| 8.0–10.0 | A | Best-in-class; competitive advantage |
| 6.5–7.9 | B | Good; minor refinements needed |
| 5.0–6.4 | C | Functional; material improvements required |
| 3.5–4.9 | D | Weak; actively losing conversions |
| 1.0–3.4 | F | Critical; immediate intervention required |

### 16.1.2 Dimension Definitions

Each dimension maps to a specific audit discipline and measures a distinct quality that affects conversion, search visibility, or brand perception.

| Dimension | Source Audit | What It Measures |
|-----------|-------------|------------------|
| **UX (Simplicity)** | UX Audit (Sec. 4–5) | Navigation clarity, information architecture, decision-path integrity, mobile experience, first-screen impact |
| **Visual Design** | Visual Design Audit (Sec. 3, 6) | Color system consistency, typography hierarchy, image quality, component discipline, mobile rendering |
| **Copywriting** | Copywriting Audit (Sec. 6) | Headline strength, CTA clarity, body copy depth, brand voice consistency, proof element quality |
| **SEO** | SEO Audit (Sec. 7) | Title tag uniqueness, meta descriptions, content indexability, schema markup, keyword targeting |
| **Brand/Positioning** | Brand Audit (Sec. 11) | Category clarity, differentiation strength, emotional signal, competitive moat expression, messaging consistency |
| **Trust** | Trust Audit (Sec. 13) | Social proof volume and quality, authority signals, risk reversal, testimonial specificity, verification markers |

Every score in the following tables traces back to specific findings documented in the referenced chapters. There is no subjective padding.

### 16.2.2 Homepage: 4.8/10 — "Survives on Its Service Cards"

The homepage carries the heaviest burden on the site — 80%+ of traffic lands here — and it scores below 5.0. The visual design audit rated the missing hero image as a "catastrophic missed opportunity." The SEO audit flagged Bali as underused in headlines and no price anchoring in the hero. Trust scores highest at 6.0, driven by strong stats (560+ villas, 12,000+ guests) and the Adriano origin story — the best copy on the site, scoring 8/10, but buried mid-page where fewer than 30% of mobile visitors ever scroll.

The diagnosis: the homepage is a trust-rich page wrapped in a confusing first impression. Visitors who scroll deep enough find reasons to believe, but too many bounce before they get there.

### 16.2.3 Fine Dining: 5.8/10 — "The Strongest Page on the Site"

Fine Dining is the highest-scoring page on myCHEF.id, and it is the only page that approaches a B grade. The UX audit gave it 7.7/10 — the strongest user journey on the site, with a 9/10 first-screen score. The hero is crystal clear: "Private Chef Bali — Fine Dining Tasting Menu in Your Villa" with IDR 2,200,000++ pricing displayed immediately. The visual design audit rated the dark overlay hero treatment as the site's best moment. Copy scores 7.0 due to strong "Built For" occasion targeting and price anchoring against Seminyak restaurants. Trust scores 7.0 because the high price point is justified by the Michelin-trained credential and detailed process explanation.

The page's weakness is SEO: it scores 2.0 because the /fine-dining URL currently redirects to the homepage, rendering it a soft 404 with duplicate title tags. Google cannot index content that does not exist as a unique page. Brand scores 4.0 because the Michelin story — the single strongest differentiator — remains buried rather than featured above the fold.

### 16.2.4 Catering: 5.6/10 — "Good Content, Orphan Page"

The Catering page scored 5.6/10, making it the third-strongest page on the domain. The UX audit rated it 7.3/10 for strong first-screen clarity: "Villa Catering Bali. Chef, Staff & Setup Included." at IDR 450K per person. The visual design audit gave it 7.0 for the italic serif secondary heading — an editorial touch the audit recommends replicating across all service pages. Copywriting scored 6.3, with "A real chef grills at your villa" identified as the standout line. Trust at 6.0 reflects solid social proof integration.

The critical finding from the SEO audit: /catering is an orphan page. Despite being the only unique service page on the domain outside the homepage, it receives zero internal links. No navigation element, no homepage card, no footer link points directly to it. Google cannot distribute authority to a page it cannot reach through the link graph. SEO at 3.0 reflects the page's orphan status and missed keyword opportunities.

### 16.2.5 Villa Chef & In-Villa Service: 4.3/10 Each — "Confused and Orphaned"

Both pages score an identical 4.3/10 but for different reasons. Villa Chef suffers from identity confusion: the UX audit found it overlaps visually and textually with /catering, sharing hero imagery and targeting similar use cases. Copy scores 4.7 because the "CATERING" subheadline on a page about personal chefs is a categorization error. Design scores 6.0 — functional but undifferentiated. The page needs either strategic merging with /catering or radical differentiation.

In-Villa Service scores lower on design (5.0) because it has no hero image — just a heading on a cream background. The visual audit called it "visually orphaned." Copy scores 5.3 because service cards are clear but pricing, use-case scenarios, and bundling with food services are all missing. SEO scores 2.0 because the page redirects to homepage content. Both pages represent unrealized potential: they address real service lines with genuine demand, but neither exists as a unique, indexable page.

### 16.2.6 Events: 4.1/10 — "Catastrophically Thin"

The Events page is the weakest non-redirect page on the site at 4.1/10. The copywriting audit was direct: the page contains approximately 30 words of copy total. The headline "Bali Event Catering & Planning. Weddings, Retreats, Celebrations." and subhead "From 20 to 200 guests. We cook, serve, and clean." constitute the entire content layer. There is no process description, no pricing (unlike Fine Dining and Catering which both display starting prices), no event type details, no staffing ratios, no timeline guidance, no gallery references, and no event-specific testimonials.

Design scores 7.0 because the visual framework is sound — the page looks like a myCHEF page even with no content. UX scores 5.7 because the structure exists but is hollow. Brand scores 3.0 because the page does not communicate whether myCHEF "caters events" or "produces experiences" — a critical positioning distinction. Trust at 4.0 reflects the absence of event-specific social proof. The SEO audit found the page is a soft 404, returning homepage content, which accounts for the 2.0 SEO score.

### 16.2.7 Contact: 5.7/10 — "Functional and Well-Designed"

The Contact page scores 5.7/10, the second-highest on the site. UX at 7.7 reflects the smart four-concierge routing system — visitors reach a specialist (Sofia, Daniel, Olivia, Marco) rather than a generic form. The "Speak to the right person" headline is personable and efficient. Design at 6.0 is clean with the poolside hero image. Copy at 6.3 because the subheadline sets expectations well: "Four people lead the four services."

The weakness is Brand at 5.0 — the highest brand score of any page, yet still only average. The brand audit noted that named concierges with personality are a competitive advantage, but the current implementation uses generic support labels rather than the full concierge names and photos described in the brand brief. SEO at 2.0 reflects duplicate title tags and redirect behavior. Trust at 7.0 is driven by the "WhatsApp online now" indicator and clear response-time promises.

### 16.2.8 Book: 4.5/10 — "The Worst-Looking Conversion Page"

The Book page scores 4.5/10 and earns the distinction of being the worst-designed conversion page on the site. Design scored 4.0 — the lowest of any non-redirect page. The visual audit found white cards on cream background (#F5F3EF vs #FFFFFF) creating "barely perceptible contrast." Five cards in a 3+2 grid create an orphan in the second row. Icons are inconsistent — chef hat vs. plate vs. people vs. wine glass vs. building — with no unified style system. WhatsApp green buttons dominate visually, "screaming 'chat app,' not 'premium booking.'" UX at 6.0 is functional — the five service cards are clear — but the BOOK button adds an unnecessary intermediate step before WhatsApp. Copy at 6.3 is adequate but the CTAs are too committal ("Book" vs. "Get Quote") for early-journey visitors.

### 16.2.9 Staffing: 1.1/10 — "A Redirect Masquerading as a Page"

The Staffing page is a scoring outlier at 1.1/10, the only F-grade on the site. It does not exist as a page. The URL /staffing redirects to /contact with zero context — a user clicking "Staffing" in the navigation sees a generic contact page with no staffing information, no service descriptions, no rates, no vetting process. The UX audit mapped this as a Critical Fail Point for B2B users: hotel managers and restaurant operators need staffing details before contacting, and the redirect kills credibility with decision-makers who expect substance.

Every scored dimension reflects the absence of content. UX at 1.7 acknowledges the Contact page is functional but captures the broken journey integrity. SEO at 1.0 because the redirect is treated as a soft 404. Brand at 1.0 because a redirect communicates "we do not take this service seriously." Trust at 1.0 because B2B users encountering a redirect lose confidence in the entire brand. Design and Copy are N/A — there is nothing to evaluate. The staffing redirect is not a minor issue; it is a declaration of operational neglect on a service line that the brand brief identifies as strategically important.
