# UX Feedback: myCHEF.id — From the Perspective of Priya & Raj
## Indian-American Destination Wedding Couple | 120 Guests | Uluwatu Villa | Budget IDR 150-250M

---

## Executive Summary

We are Priya (30, Management Consultant) and Raj (32, Software Engineer). We are planning a 3-day Indian wedding celebration at a cliff-front villa in Uluwatu with 120 guests, and we need a catering partner who can execute Indian/Western/Balinese fusion cuisine at a level that will impress our families — many of whom are flying from India and the US specifically for this. Food is the centerpiece of our celebration. A wedding planner in Bali referred us to myCHEF.id.

We spent 30+ minutes browsing every section of the myCHEF.id website: homepage, Fine Dining, Events, Contact, gallery, and inquiry forms. Here is our honest, unfiltered feedback.

## What Worked Well

### 1. The "200+ Weddings" Social Proof
Seeing "200+ Weddings across Bali's finest villas" immediately gave us a confidence boost. Combined with the "8+ Years in Bali" and "12,000+ Happy Guests" stats on the homepage, this is a company that has real track record. The testimonial from "Sarah & Michael, Villa Wedding, Uluwatu" hit close to home — same area, similar event type.

### 2. The Planning Process (5 Steps)
The structured planning process is exactly what an anxious bride managing vendors from 10,000 miles away needs:
- **Inquiry** → tell them what we need
- **Planning Call** → video call capability! This is critical for us
- **Custom Proposal** → detailed menu, staffing, pricing
- **Event Coordination** → confirms details, arranges rentals
- **Execution** → they show up and deliver

This framework signals professionalism and reduces our fear of "things falling through the cracks."

### 3. Full-Service Rentals & Staffing
The Rentals & Staffing section (chefs, bartenders, waiters, glassware, tables/chairs, sound systems, decor, live stations) shows this is a one-stop shop. For a 120-guest wedding, we don't want to coordinate 8 different vendors. The "Build Your Event Team" concept is smart.

### 4. The Inquiry Form — Especially Budget Tiers
The Events inquiry form asks the RIGHT questions. The budget dropdown (IDR 100-200M, IDR 200M+) aligns perfectly with our budget range. The guest count tier goes up to "200+" which covers our 120 guests comfortably. The service checkboxes let us specify exactly what we need. The "Additional Details" field is where we'll write our novel about Indian cuisine requirements.

### 5. WhatsApp Integration
The WhatsApp chat button on every page is genuinely useful. For international couples, WhatsApp is how we communicate with our Bali wedding planner. Being able to reach myCHEF the same way feels natural and immediate.

### 6. Visual Design — Premium, Not Cheap
The dark theme with gold accents, the quality food photography, and the clean typography all communicate "luxury service." This matters for a formal wedding where our families expect white-glove service. The staff photos show people in formal attire — reassuring.

### 7. "Custom Quoted" for Events
Events being custom-quoted (not priced on the site) is appropriate for weddings. It forces a conversation where we can explain our Indian cuisine needs. The Fine Dining pricing (IDR 2.2M+ per guest) actually makes us feel better about our IDR 150-250M budget for 120 guests — there's room.

---

## What Was Missing (Especially Critical for Indian Wedding Needs)

### 1. **ZERO Evidence of Multi-Cultural or Indian Cuisine Experience** (Our #1 Fear)
This is the single biggest red flag. We searched every page, every FAQ, every menu description. Here is what we found about non-Italian cuisine:
- The homepage leads with "Italian Fine Dining"
- The Fine Dining page is exclusively Italian (pasta, crudo, formaggio)
- Under Private Chefs in Rentals & Staffing: "Michelin-trained and experienced villa chefs for any cuisine and event size" — ONE vague line
- Dietary FAQ mentions "halal" as an accommodation
- **No Indian cuisine. No Asian fusion. No mention of spice levels, vegetarian thalis, tandoor, biryani, or any Indian dishes.**

For an Indian wedding where 80% of our guests are Indian and expect authentic flavors, this is terrifying. We can't be their experiment. We need evidence they've done this before.

### 2. **No Multi-Day Wedding Content**
Our wedding is 3 days (Sangeet, Wedding Ceremony, Reception). The site treats "wedding" as a single dinner event. There's no content about:
- Multi-day celebrations
- Different food styles for different events (e.g., casual grazing for Sangeet, formal plated for Reception)
- Breakfast/lunch service for wedding guests staying at the villa
- Late-night after-party snacks

### 3. **Gallery is Broken — 5 of 6 Images Don't Load**
The "Events We've Brought to Life" gallery on the Events page shows only 1 working image (a beautiful cliffside wedding) and 5 broken image placeholders. For a visual service like catering, this is unacceptable. We need to see food, setups, large groups, service in action.

### 4. **No Case Studies or Real Wedding Stories**
Beyond the one-line testimonial from Sarah & Michael, there are no detailed wedding case studies. No "here's how we catered a 150-guest Indian wedding at Villa XYZ." No before/after photos. No menu examples for large events. No video testimonials.

### 5. **No Sample Event Menus or Pricing Guidance**
The Fine Dining page has detailed Italian menus with pricing. The Events page has nothing — no sample wedding menus, no per-guest pricing guidance for events, no package tiers. We have no idea if our IDR 150-250M budget is realistic until we fill out a form and wait.

### 6. **No Mention of Collaboration with External Caterers**
Our wedding planner suggested we might bring a specialist Indian chef from Jakarta/Singapore for certain dishes. We need to know if myCHEF can:
- Collaborate with an external Indian chef
- Provide kitchen space and support staff
- Handle the logistics of a hybrid catering setup

The site is silent on this.

### 7. **No Tasting or Preview Option Mentioned**
One of our key decision criteria is a tasting option. We'll be in Bali 2 months before the wedding — can we do a tasting? Is there a cost? How far in advance? Not mentioned anywhere.

### 8. **Missing FAQ Topics**
The FAQ covers: booking advance, service areas, dietary restrictions, cancellation. Missing:
- Can you handle [X] cuisine?
- Do you offer tastings?
- Can we customize the menu?
- Can you work with our wedding planner?
- Do you handle vendor meals?
- What happens if it rains? (outdoor contingency)
- Do you provide children's meals?

### 9. **"Response within 24 hours" is Slow for Anxious Couples**
The Contact page says "response within 24 hours." For a detail-obsessed bride planning from New York, 24 hours feels like an eternity when you're trying to compare 3 vendors. The WhatsApp "response within 1 hour" is better, but the email/primary form promise should be faster.

### 10. **No Indian or South Asian Imagery**
Every person in every photo appears to be white/Western. The food is European. The wedding couple in the hero image is white. We understand Bali's tourism demographics, but seeing zero diversity in imagery makes us wonder — do they even WANT our business?

## What Was Confusing or Frustrating

### 1. **"Italian Fine Dining" as the Primary Brand Identity**
The site opens with Italian fine dining, and that's the primary service highlighted. For a couple looking for wedding catering, having to dig through 3 navigation layers to find the Events section feels like we're not the target customer. It would be better if the homepage equally featured Events/Weddings alongside Fine Dining.

### 2. **Navigation Inconsistency**
The "Contact Us" link in the main nav sometimes scrolls to the form on the current page, sometimes goes to the dedicated Contact page. The Events page has its own embedded form, but the Contact page has a DIFFERENT form with different fields. It wasn't immediately clear which form to use.

### 3. **Broken Images Throughout**
Beyond the Events gallery, multiple images on the Events page didn't load:
- Retreats card image (broken)
- Corporate Events card image (broken) 
- Private Parties card image (broken)
- Birthdays card image (broken)
- 5 of 6 gallery images (broken)

This many broken images on a premium service website is a major credibility hit.

### 4. **No Clear Path from Homepage to Wedding Content**
If you're a wedding couple landing on the homepage, you need to: scroll past Fine Dining, scroll past Villa Catering, find Events, click it, then scroll to find the Wedding subsection. There should be a direct "Planning a Wedding?" CTA on the homepage.

### 5. **"Custom Quoted" Without Any Price Anchors**
We understand events are custom-priced, but giving us ZERO price guidance means we have to fill out a form and engage in a back-and-forth just to know if we're in the same ballpark. A simple "Wedding catering typically ranges from IDR X-Y per guest" would save everyone time.

## Would We Shortlist myCHEF.id for Our Wedding?

### Answer: **Maybe — but with serious reservations.**

**What's working in their favor:**
- 200+ weddings in Bali (they've done this at scale)
- Full-service capability (food + staff + rentals)
- 5-step planning process with video call
- Uluwatu wedding testimonial (same area as ours)
- Comprehensive inquiry form
- 8+ years in Bali (they understand the villa ecosystem)
- Budget tiers align with our range
- "Any cuisine" mention under Private Chefs

**What's working against them:**
- No evidence of Indian cuisine experience (our #1 requirement)
- No multi-cultural event portfolio
- Broken gallery images
- No multi-day wedding content
- No tasting option mentioned
- No sample event menus or pricing
- Italian-centric brand identity feels mismatched
- No diversity in imagery

**Our likely next step:** We would fill out the Events inquiry form and specifically ask about Indian cuisine experience in the "Additional Details" field. But honestly, if there were another catering company in Bali that even *mentioned* Indian weddings on their website, we'd contact them first. We'd probably reach out to myCHEF only because our wedding planner recommended them — not because the website convinced us.

---

## What ONE Thing Would Most Improve Our Confidence?

> **A dedicated "Multi-Cultural Weddings" page with evidence of Indian/Asian/ fusion cuisine events, featuring real photos, sample menus, and testimonials from non-Western couples.**

Specifically, we need to see:
- Photos of Indian, Asian, or multi-cultural weddings they've catered
- A sample Indian/Balinese fusion menu for a 100+ guest event
- A testimonial from an Indian or South Asian couple
- Explicit language like "Our chefs are experienced in Indian, Asian, Middle Eastern, and European cuisines. We regularly collaborate with specialist chefs for authentic regional dishes."
- A note about accommodating external chefs/caterers for specialty items

Right now, the website feels like it was built for a wealthy European couple wanting a romantic Italian dinner in their Bali villa. That's a completely different customer from us. The events capability is there operationally, but it's not *communicated* in a way that makes an Indian wedding couple feel confident.
