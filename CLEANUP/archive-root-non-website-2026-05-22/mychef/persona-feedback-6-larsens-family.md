# UX Feedback: The Larsens — Nordic Family Reunion (14 people, 12 days, Berawa)

**Persona:** Norwegian family, 3 related families (parents 50s, adult children 25-30, younger kids 5-10). Total 14 people. First time in Bali. Budget: IDR 30-40M for 12 days (~$1,800-2,400 USD). Looking for fresh, healthy, family-style meals.

**Date of Review:** 2025
**Pages Reviewed:** Homepage, Villa Catering, Contact Us

---

## Numerical Ratings (1-10)

### Overall First Impression: 6/10

The site looks polished and professional — dark hero with an elegant 3D globe, clean typography, gold accents. As a Norwegian family reunion group, my first thought was: "This looks like it's for honeymooners and anniversary dinners." The homepage hero says "Private Chef, Fine Dining & Event Experiences" — immediately signals luxury, not family practicality. The one testimonial visible is from "Sarah & James, London" about their anniversary. I don't feel like this site was built with families like mine in mind.

The stats are reassuring (500+ villas, 12,000+ guests, 4.9 rating, 8+ years) but they feel generic. The "How It Works" section with 3 steps is clear but again feels like it's written for couples booking a romantic dinner, not a family of 14 planning 12 days of meals.

### Pricing Clarity and Transparency: 4/10

**This is where I started getting anxious.** I need to know: how much will this cost me for 14 people for 12 days? Can I compare it to eating out? The pricing structure is confusing:

1. **IDR 600,000/hour** — starting rate, but "actual rate depends on experience level, menu complexity, and schedule." So the real rate could be much higher.
2. **Daily Chef: IDR 4.8M/day** — but this is chef time only, groceries are "billed separately." And this only covers breakfast & dinner, not lunch.
3. **Weekly Villa Chef: IDR 28M/week** — again, "Chef time + groceries billed separately." This is the plan I'd need, but for 12 days I'd need 2 weeks = IDR 56M just for the chef.
4. **Long-Stay Chef: Custom Quote** — this would be my best option, but it requires reaching out.

The "Transparent Pricing, No Surprises" heading feels ironic because there are nothing but surprises. Every price has an asterisk. The FAQ confirms: "Groceries are billed separately. You only pay the actual cost of groceries plus a small shopping fee." What does "small shopping fee" mean? IDR 50K? IDR 500K? Per day? No answer.

The pricing examples are useful but not at my scale:
- "Family of 4, breakfast only: ~IDR 1.2M/day" — I'm 14 people, 3 meals. Extrapolating gives me no confidence.
- "Couple, full-day chef: ~IDR 4.8M/day" — Couple. I'm 14.
- "Large group BBQ dinner: ~IDR 3.5M/event" — Finally a large group mention, but just one meal.

**Bottom line: I cannot calculate my total cost without contacting them.** This is frustrating because as a systematic comparison shopper, I need numbers before I commit to a conversation.

### Value Proposition vs. Eating Out: 3/10

**This is a critical failure.** The site never once compares the cost to eating out. Let me do the math myself:

**Eating out in Berawa/Canggu:**
- Decent restaurant: ~IDR 100-150K/person/meal
- 14 people x 3 meals x 12 days = ~IDR 50-75M total
- Warung/local food: ~IDR 50-80K/person/meal = ~IDR 25-40M total
- Plus transport, tips, waiting time, finding places that seat 14...

**myCHEF Villa Catering (my estimate):**
- Weekly chef: IDR 28M/week x 2 weeks = IDR 56M (chef only!)
- Groceries for 14 people x 12 days: ~IDR 15-20M
- Additional staff for 14 people: ???
- **Total estimate: IDR 70-80M minimum**

**My budget is IDR 30-40M for 12 days.**

The service is at least 2x my budget, possibly 3x. The website never acknowledges this reality. There's no "budget-friendly options" section, no "how to make this work for your budget," no "groceries-only service where you cook yourself." Just premium pricing that silently excludes families like mine.

If the website had a simple comparison chart — "Eating out for 14 people: IDR 60-80M vs. Private chef: IDR 70-90M vs. Our budget meal plan: IDR 35-45M" — I would at least feel respected as a visitor. Instead, I feel like the wrong audience for this website.

### Family-Friendly Signals: 6/10

**What's good:**
- The Villa Catering page explicitly mentions "Family Dining" as a service category ("Family-style sharing plates that bring everyone together")
- "Kids Meals" is listed as a service ("Nutritious, appealing meals designed for young palates")
- The hero image on Villa Catering shows a family with children eating together
- FAQ confirms: "Yes, all our chefs are experienced in preparing nutritious, kid-friendly meals. We can also accommodate picky eaters and food allergies."

**What's missing:**
- No testimonial from a large family or family reunion
- No photos of large family groups actually dining together
- No mention of age-appropriate menu options or portion sizes for kids vs. adults
- No mention of high chairs, kid-friendly tableware, or early meal times for young children
- The "Family Dining" service card has no detail about what "family-style sharing plates" actually means — is it Indonesian rijsttafel? Western-style? BBQ?

I saw the word "family" but I never felt like the site truly understood what a family reunion with 5-10 year olds looks like.

### Healthy/Fresh Cooking Evidence: 7/10

This is actually one of the stronger areas for my needs:
- "Healthy Meals" service explicitly listed: "Wellness-focused menus with organic ingredients, superfoods, and dietary accommodations"
- " sourced fresh from local Balinese markets and premium suppliers" — mentioned in the Why myCHEF section
- "Fresh tropical breakfasts, smoothie bowls" — Breakfast Chefs description
- Dietary accommodations confirmed: "Our chefs are experienced with vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free, dairy-free, halal, keto, and other dietary needs"
- Grocery shopping service means fresh ingredients daily

However, there's a disconnect: the site talks about "Michelin-level technique" and "fine dining" in multiple places. As a health-conscious Nordic family, I want simple, fresh, clean food — not foam and reductions. The branding leans too heavily toward luxury and not enough toward wholesome. I want to see words like "simple," "clean," "light," "Nordic-inspired," "farm-to-table" — not "Michelin-level."

### Operational Clarity (What's Included): 5/10

The "What's Included" section is clear about the basics:
- Chef's time and expertise
- Menu planning and customization
- Meal preparation and service
- Kitchen cleanup

And clear about additional costs:
- Groceries and ingredients
- Additional staff (waiters, helpers)
- Special equipment or rentals

**But critical questions remain unanswered:**
- How many hours per day does the chef work for the weekly plan? (Is it 6 hours? 10 hours?)
- Does the chef need a separate place to stay for long-term bookings?
- What happens on the chef's days off? (Long-stay FAQ mentions "replacement chef coverage" which is good)
- How does grocery budgeting work? Can I set a daily/weekly grocery budget?
- Who decides the menu? Can I approve it in advance?
- What time does the chef arrive and leave?
- Is breakfast prepared fresh or pre-prepped the night before?

The "Grocery budget management" is listed under Long-Stay Chef but not explained. For a family of 14, this would be essential.

### Ease of Comparison Shopping: 3/10

**Very difficult.** I cannot easily compare:
1. The three meal plans (Daily vs. Weekly vs. Long-Stay) because the features and pricing structures are different
2. This service vs. competitors — no competitor comparison at all
3. This service vs. eating out — no comparison provided
4. Cost per person per meal — I'd have to calculate this myself, and the chef time vs. groceries split makes it complicated

There are no package deals, no "family reunion special," no group discounts mentioned. For 14 people, I would expect some acknowledgment of group pricing. The pricing examples max out at "Large group BBQ dinner" with no mention of group size.

The "Get a Custom Quote" buttons are everywhere — the site essentially forces you to inquire rather than self-serve. As a systematic comparison shopper, this is frustrating. I want to compare on my own time, not start a sales conversation.

### Mobile Experience: 6/10

The site appears to be responsive. The navigation collapses to a hamburger menu on smaller screens. The service cards stack vertically. The forms are usable on mobile. The gold and dark color scheme translates well to mobile. WhatsApp chat button is visible and accessible. However, the homepage hero with the 3D globe animation may be heavy on mobile data and battery. The Villa Catering page contact form with all its fields would be tedious to fill out on a small screen. The gallery images on Villa Catering were broken on desktop, which likely persists on mobile.

## Detailed Feedback

### What Worked Well

1. **Clear WhatsApp contact option** — "Response within 1 hour" with a direct WhatsApp link is exactly what I want as a first-time visitor to Bali. No complicated forms, no waiting for email replies. Straight to chat.

2. **Service categories are well-organized** — Breaking services into Breakfast Chefs, Daily Meals, BBQ & Grill, Family Dining, Healthy Meals, Kids Meals, Meal Prep, and Grocery Shopping shows they understand different needs. This gave me confidence that they CAN handle our situation.

3. **Kids meals FAQ** — The direct answer "Yes, all our chefs are experienced in preparing nutritious, kid-friendly meals. We can also accommodate picky eaters and food allergies" was reassuring. With 5-10 year olds in the group, this matters.

4. **Dietary accommodations** — Listing vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free, dairy-free, halal, and keto explicitly gives me confidence that "healthy Nordic-style" (lots of fish, vegetables, whole grains, minimal processing) would be accommodated.

5. **Long-stay specialization** — The FAQ about hiring for one month with "replacement chef coverage for days off" shows operational maturity. For a 12-day stay, this is reassuring.

6. **"Grocery shopping and budget management"** — Mentioned in the Villa Chef Rental section. If this means I can set a daily grocery budget and the chef works within it, that's exactly what I need. But it's not explained in detail.

### What Was Missing

1. **A price comparison with eating out** — This is the single biggest gap. For a family reunion calculating whether to hire a chef vs. eat out 3x/day, this comparison is essential. A simple table would solve this.

2. **Sample family-style menus** — I want to see what a family dinner for 10-15 people actually looks like. What's on the menu? Grilled fish with vegetables? Nasi campur? Mediterranean sharing plates? Without seeing actual menu examples, I can't judge whether the food matches our family's tastes.

3. **A testimonial from a large family or family reunion** — The one testimonial is from an anniversary couple. I want to hear from a family of 10+ who stayed for 10+ days. Did the kids eat the food? Was it worth it? Did they feel the cost was justified?

4. **Group size pricing examples** — The examples top out at "Family of 4" and "Couple." Where is the "Large family of 10-16, full-day chef" example? That would be my scenario.

5. **All-inclusive pricing option** — The groceries-are-separate model creates uncertainty. I would much prefer a per-person-per-day all-inclusive price that includes chef time, groceries, and service. Something like "IDR 350K/person/day including all meals, groceries, and chef service." Then I can calculate: 14 x IDR 350K x 12 = IDR 58.8M. Still over budget but at least transparent.

6. **Budget-friendly options or packages** — If the weekly plan (IDR 28M/week) is too expensive, are there alternatives? What about a chef for dinner only? Breakfast only? 3 days a week instead of every day? The "Flexible schedules" bullet point mentions "breakfast only, full-day, or everything in between" but there are no prices for partial coverage.

7. **Per-person, per-meal cost breakdown** — For comparison shopping, I need to know the cost per person per meal. If I can't calculate this myself, the site should do it for me.

8. **Photos of actual food / menu items** — The gallery section on Villa Catering shows broken images (alt text visible: "Villa dining 1", "Villa dining 2", etc.). This is a major trust issue. I can't see what the food looks like.

9. **Cancellation policy** — Listed in the Contact page "Common Questions" but I couldn't expand it to read the answer. For a family reunion where plans might change, this is important.

10. **Areas served** — "Which areas of Bali do you serve?" is in the Common Questions but the answer wasn't visible. I need to confirm they serve Berawa.

### What Was Confusing or Frustrating

1. **Two different contact forms** — The Villa Catering page has a detailed form with fields for Stay Duration, Group Size, Meals Needed (checkboxes for Breakfast/Lunch/Dinner/BBQ/Special Events), and Dietary Requirements. The Contact Us page has a generic form with just Name, Email, Phone, Villa/Location, How did you hear about us, and Message. **The Villa Catering form is much better for my needs, but I only found it by scrolling to the bottom of the Villa Catering page.** If I went straight to Contact Us, I'd get a less useful form. This inconsistency is confusing.

2. **"Chef time + groceries billed separately" on every plan** — This phrase appears on the Daily Chef plan (IDR 4.8M/day), the Weekly plan (IDR 28M/week), and is confirmed in the FAQ. It's never quantified. What percentage of total cost is typically groceries? Is it 30%? 50%? Give me a rule of thumb.

3. **"Grocery shopping included" vs. "groceries billed separately"** — On the Daily Chef plan, the feature list says "Grocery shopping included" but the fine print says "Chef time + groceries billed separately." So the SERVICE of shopping is included, but the COST of groceries is not. This is a distinction that took me a moment to parse. For non-native English speakers (Norse family), this could be genuinely confusing.

4. **The "Book a Villa Chef" CTA goes nowhere specific** — Clicking "Book a Villa Chef" on the Villa Catering hero just scrolls down to the form. It doesn't feel like a booking action, more like a "scroll down to learn more."

5. **Fine Dining (IDR 2.2M per guest) is prominently featured** — On the homepage and in the navigation, Fine Dining is the first and most prominent service. At IDR 2.2M per person, that's IDR 30.8M for one dinner for 14 people — almost my entire 12-day budget. This pricing actually makes me question whether the Villa Catering service will also be unaffordable. The luxury positioning of Fine Dining bleeds over and creates doubt about Villa Catering pricing.

6. **Gallery images are broken** — Multiple "Villa dining" images show only alt text, no actual photos. This destroys trust. If they can't maintain their website images, can they manage a 12-day catering operation?

7. **No "back to top" button** — The Villa Catering page is very long. Scrolling back to the navigation requires manual effort.

8. **"Get a Custom Quote" / "Enquire Now" / "Talk to Us" / "Find Your Chef" — too many CTA variations** — Each plan has a differently-named button. It would be simpler if they all said the same thing, like "Get a Quote."

## The Big Question: Would You Book? Why or Why Not?

**No, I would not book — at least not without significant changes or a custom negotiation.**

Here's my reasoning as a practical, cost-conscious Norwegian family reunion planner:

**The math doesn't work:**
- My budget: IDR 30-40M for 12 days (IDR 2.5-3.3M/day for 14 people)
- Weekly chef plan alone: IDR 28M/week x 2 weeks = IDR 56M
- Plus groceries: ~IDR 15-20M for 14 people x 12 days
- Total minimum: IDR 70-80M
- **That's 2x my budget at minimum.**

Even the Daily Chef option at IDR 4.8M/day (chef only, breakfast & dinner only, no lunch) for 12 days = IDR 57.6M plus groceries. Still way over.

**The emotional journey:**
1. I arrived at the site feeling hopeful — "Private chef for our villa, that would be perfect for a family reunion"
2. I saw the Villa Catering page and felt more hopeful — "Family Dining, Kids Meals, Healthy Meals, Grocery Shopping — this sounds tailored for us"
3. I saw IDR 600K/hour and thought "That's manageable..."
4. Then I saw the meal plans and started doing the math, and my stomach dropped
5. I kept scrolling looking for group discounts, budget options, or a "how this compares to eating out" section
6. I found nothing. Just "Get a Custom Quote" buttons
7. I left feeling that this service isn't for families like mine

**What WOULD make me book:**
If the "Talk to Us" / custom quote process revealed that:
- They offer a "family reunion package" at a group discount
- The long-stay rate for 12 days is significantly lower than the weekly rate x2
- Groceries can be kept to a modest budget (IDR 50-70K/person/meal)
- They offer partial service (e.g., dinner only, or every other day) at proportional pricing
- Total all-in could be negotiated to IDR 35-45M

But the website gives me zero confidence that any of this is possible. It reads as a premium-only service, and I'm not a premium-only customer.

---

## What One Thing Would Most Improve Your Confidence?

**A single, honest price comparison showing "Total Cost for Your Group" with a slider or calculator.**

Specifically, I want to see:

> **"For a group of 14 staying 12 days in Berawa:"**
> - Eating out 3x/day at restaurants: ~IDR 60-80M
> - Eating at local warungs: ~IDR 30-45M
> - Our Villa Chef service (all-inclusive): ~IDR 45-55M
> - Includes: Chef, all groceries, menu planning, kitchen cleanup
> - Price per person per day: ~IDR 270-330K

Even if the number is above my stated budget, the transparency and direct comparison would build trust. Right now I feel like I'm being guided into a sales conversation without enough information to make an informed decision. A price calculator or comparison table would change that completely.

Alternatively, if the service truly CAN'T work within my IDR 30-40M budget, the site should say so upfront: **"Our villa catering service typically starts at IDR X per day for groups of 10+. For budget-friendly options, we recommend [alternative]."** This would be honest and save me time.
