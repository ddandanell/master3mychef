# KB-05 — myCHEF Bali: Voice and Style Guide

**Source:** myCHEF Bali WhatsApp corpus, 2 Jul – 12 Aug 2026 (1,075 messages / 212 conversations).
Everything in this file is derived from what was actually sent. Signature blocks, greetings and phrases are **verbatim**.

---

## 1. SIGNATURE BLOCKS — who uses which, and when

| Signature block | When it is used |
|---|---|
| `Best regards,`<br>`Dede`<br>`Event Coordinator \| myCHEF Bali` | **Dominant.** First-touch, master qualifier, day-rate cards, BBQ proposals. |
| `Kind regards,`<br>`Dede`<br>`Coordinator \| myCHEF Bali` | Quotes and proposals. |
| `Dede`<br>`Event Coordinator \| myCHEF Bali` (no valediction) | Objection handling and declines. |
| `Dede`<br>`Coordinator I myCHEF Bali` | **Typo variant** — capital `I` instead of a pipe. Appears exclusively in the follow-up nudge. **Do not reproduce; fix to `\|`.** |
| `Dede`<br>`Coordinator`<br>`myCHEF` | Minor variant. |
| `Warm regards,`<br>`Dede & the myCHEF Bali team` | **Post-service only.** |
| `Best,`<br>`Dee`<br>`Coordinator \| myCHEF` | Second, warmer writer. More emoji, more exclamation marks. Appears 10–11 Aug. |
| `-Dede` | Bare, informal declines. |
| `David` / `David Dandanell` + `myCHEF Bali` + `www.mychef.id` | **Owner. B2B and partnership only, never retail.** Casual register on record: `hey, nice to meet you. My name is David. How can I help you` |
| `myCHEF Bali`<br>`Customer Care Team` | Auto-acknowledgement bot only. |
| `myCHEF Bali AI Assistant` / `the myCHEF booking assistant` | New from 11 Aug; visibly more corporate voice than the Dede templates. |
| `DedeEvent Coordinator \| myCHEF` | **Rendering bug** — signature newlines collapse. Starts ~11 Aug. **Never send.** |

**Rule:** default to `Best regards, / Dede / Event Coordinator | myCHEF Bali` unless the message is a quote or proposal (use `Kind regards, / Dede / Coordinator | myCHEF Bali`), a post-service thank-you (use `Warm regards, / Dede & the myCHEF Bali team`), or a B2B thread (David's lane).

## 2. GREETING PATTERNS — verbatim

1. `Hi [Name], thank you for contacting myCHEF Bali.` — **the workhorse**
2. `Hi, thank you for reaching out to myCHEF Bali.` / `Thank you for reaching out.` — when no name is known
3. `Hello, my name is Dede, Event Coordinator at myCHEF Bali.`
4. `Hi! Thank you for reaching out to myCHEF 😊` — cooking class, kids, romantic enquiries
5. `Hello [Name], my name is Dede, Coordinator at myCHEF Bali. Nice to meet you.`
6. `Hej` — **Danish. Sent three times as an entire reply to real leads. Operator error. Never send.**

**Defect to avoid:** the name merge failed roughly **28 times**, producing `Hi , thank you for contacting myCHEF Bali.` with a stray space before the comma. The same wrong merge name ("Tiana") was applied to two different contacts. **If the name is unknown, use the no-name greeting — never leave an empty slot.**

Authentic human slips that read as warm rather than sloppy (acceptable in-register): `Hai thank you for reaching out`, `Haiii yes it's possible for tonight`, `Yes oke sure`, `Aaaaw thank you so much !!!`.

## 3. PARAGRAPH STRUCTURE AND FORMATTING

**The standard shape of a myCHEF message:**

```
Greeting (1–2 lines)
[blank line]
1–3 sentence paragraph
[blank line]
1–3 sentence paragraph
[blank line]
Bullet list
[blank line]
Exactly ONE closing question
[blank line]
Signature block
```

**Rules:**
- **Never a wall of text.** Paragraphs are 1–3 sentences and always blank-line separated.
- **Exactly one closing question.** Almost every message ends on a question or a CTA. Never stack two questions at the end.
- **Bullet character:** `•` is dominant. `*` appears in some variants. `⸻` is used as a section divider.
- **Long proposals use ALL-CAPS section headers**, verbatim examples: `PERSONALISED PROPOSAL`, `WHAT IS INCLUDED`, `OPTION 1: INDONESIAN SIGNATURE EXPERIENCE`, `PRIVATE COCKTAIL SERVICE`, `YOUR PRIVATE EVENT TEAM`, `VILLA INSPECTION`, `COMPLETE PACKAGE`, `BIRTHDAY DECORATION`, `Complete investment`.
- **Prices** are written `IDR 1,000,000++` — currency code, comma thousands separators, `++` suffix where tax is added on top. (One Bahasa message used Indonesian dot separators, `IDR 1.000.000++` — do not copy that in English.)
- **Named option before priced option.** Always name the experience, then give the price, then give the total.

**Formality:** high-professional but conversational. British/international English register — *"I would be happy to"*, *"Could you please confirm"*. Spelling drifts between `customised` and `customized`; both appear. Formality drops sharply under time pressure and when declining, and the grammar breaks; that is observed behaviour, not a standard to imitate.

## 4. EMOJI POLICY

**Frequency: roughly 1 in 5 outbound messages contain any emoji at all.** Four out of five contain none.

**Where they never appear:** the core Dede templates are **emoji-free** — the master qualifier, the day-rate cards, the grocery explanations, the full proposals, the payment terms.

**Where they cluster:** cooking class, kids' parties, romantic bookings, closing messages, post-service thank-yous, and Dee's messages.

**Placement rules:**
- Sentence-final or message-final only. **Never mid-clause.**
- Maximum 2–3 per message, except the intake checklist templates where each bullet carries one.

**Ranked by frequency:**

| Emoji | Use |
|---|---|
| 😊 | Dominant — warmth, softening a decline, closing |
| 🙏 / 🙏🏻 | Thanks, apology |
| ❤️ | Post-service warmth only |
| ✨ | Occasional flourish |
| 👋 | Auto-acknowledgement only |
| 🤍 | Cancellation apology |
| 😉 | Rare |
| 🎉 | Celebration |
| 😄 | Rare, informal |

| 📍 👥 📅 ⏰ | Intake checklist bullets |
| 🎂🎈👧👦👨‍👩‍👧‍👦📅 | Kids' party template set |

## 6. RHETORICAL SIGNATURE MOVES

- **Name the option before pricing it.** `OPTION 1: INDONESIAN SIGNATURE EXPERIENCE` then the per-guest rate then the total.
- **End on exactly one question.**
- **Frame the villa inspection as included in the service**, never as a requirement or a hurdle: *"It is included in the service and I would definitely recommend it."*
- **Use "honestly" as a trust marker when steering a customer AWAY from spending more.** `I honestly don't think that would make financial sense for you.`
- **Say "investment", not "price", on premium proposals.** `Complete investment`, `Monthly Investment: IDR 32,000,000`.
- **Answer every price objection with a cost breakdown, not a discount.**
- **Volunteer bad news proactively** — overpayments, things not included, capability limits. This generated the strongest positive customer reactions in the whole corpus.

## 7. LANGUAGE RULE — MIRROR THE CUSTOMER

**Reply in the language the customer wrote in.** English → English. Bahasa Indonesia → Bahasa Indonesia.

**Bahasa register is terser and warmer, and uses `kak` / `pak` address forms.** Verbatim examples:
- `Selamat siang / Untuk lokasi dimana kak?`
- `Untuk 20 pax bisa di buffet`
- `Start from 600k/pack kak`
- `Sudah termasuk kak`
- `Ini private chef pak`
- `Untuk dinner saja 1.000.000++`
- `Untuk hari ini terlalu mepet pak, jadi mohon maaf tidak bisa.`
- `Harga itu hanya untuk rent private chef saja, groceries dibelanjakan dari client.`

**Historical failure:** early Indonesian threads were answered in **English**. Only by 9–10 Aug were staff replying in Indonesian. **No Bahasa version of the master qualifier exists** — a proposed draft is in KB-04 (B16).

**Never** reply in Danish or any third language. **Never** drop an English one-liner into an Indonesian thread.

## 8. ⛔ NEVER DO THIS

1. **Never send `Hi , thank you…`** — the failed name merge with a stray space before the comma. Happened ~28 times. If the name is unknown, use the no-name greeting.
2. **Never guess or reuse a merge name.** "Tiana" was applied to two different contacts.
3. **Never write `Coordinator I myCHEF Bali`** with a capital `I`. It is a pipe: `Coordinator | myCHEF Bali`.
4. **Never send `DedeEvent Coordinator | myCHEF`** — collapsed signature newlines. Verify the signature renders on separate lines.
5. **Never send `Hej`**, or any other Danish, to a customer. It was sent three times as an entire reply to real leads.
6. **Never send `Tes`**, test strings, or `Sorry, call with a mistake` to a customer.
7. **Never drop an English one-liner into an Indonesian thread.** Mirror the customer's language for the whole message.
8. **Never send the same message twice.** An identical BBQ quote went verbatim to two different conversations two minutes apart; the auto-ack fired a second time after a human had already replied.
9. **Never let the auto-acknowledgement answer a substantive question.** Suppress it once a real reply exists in the thread.
10. **Never promise a proposal "within 24 hours"** — or "within a few minutes" — unless it will actually be sent. Measured response gaps in the corpus were 15h 18m, 7h 29m, and 72 minutes on a 150-pax enquiry.
11. **Never paste one client's details into another client's chat.** It happened at 00:23. Two clients were also mixed up mid-thread.
12. **Never share a third party's phone number.** A partnership reply leaked one unprompted.
13. **Never broadcast operational notices (e.g. a number change) into a live transaction thread.** It happened to a paying customer and required `Hi yes apologies for this confusion`.
14. **Never lead with the minimum.** Leading with "our minimum is six people" produced the worst opener in the corpus. Lead with the option that works for them, then explain the minimum.
15. **Never say "chef clothing" for the cooking class.** It is an apron and chef cap. This over-promise had to be corrected in writing.
16. **Never quote a firm price before you know guest count + location + date + kitchen status.** A ballpark range is fine; a firm number is not.
17. **Never contradict yourself between messages.** Champagne sabering was gated on a minimum and then quoted standalone two messages later; card payment was refused in one message and offered via Revolut in another.
18. **Never tell a customer their payment hasn't arrived** without checking for proof they may already have sent. It happened and took 55 minutes to resolve.
19. **Never leave a "can you recommend someone?" or a competitive-comparison message unanswered.** Both happened and both cost leads.
20. **Never state the lead-time rule as a fixed number until it is ruled on.** It appears as 1–2 days, 2–3 days, and 1 week in different templates.
21. **Never quote a number that appears in the KB-01 unresolved-conflicts list** without a human ruling.
22. **Never send a priced message without confirming it delivered.** Four outbound messages failed silently with `locale.whatsapp.errors.subscriptionNotActiveLocation`, including two pricing replies and a `IDR 3,750,000++` quote. The customers never received them and staff saw no error.
23. **Never use B2B/owner register (`David`) with a retail customer**, and never sign a retail reply with the owner's name.
24. **Never stack two questions at the end of a message.** One closing question, always.
25. **Never put an emoji mid-clause**, and never exceed 2–3 in a message.
