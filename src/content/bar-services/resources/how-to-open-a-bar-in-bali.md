## Start with concept and feasibility

Before you sign a lease or order equipment, define your concept, target guest profile and price ladder. A beach club, a hotel lobby bar and a neighbourhood restaurant bar have different volumes, staffing models and licence requirements.

A short feasibility note should cover projected covers, average spend, beverage target margin, staffing plan and an honest view of nearby competition. This document becomes your filter for every later decision.

## The licences you need

Alcohol retail in Indonesia requires a SIUP-MB (Surat Izin Usaha Perdagangan Minuman Beralkohol) held by the venue or business entity. You will also need a TDUP (Tanda Daftar Usaha Pariwisata) tied to your tourism-business registration, plus the standard business licences such as NIB and NPWP.

Foreign investors usually operate through a PT PMA. The structure, capital requirements and sector classifications change over time, so engage a qualified local legal consultant early. MyChef can advise on sequencing and paperwork during setup projects, but we do not hold licences on your behalf.

## Layout and workflow before equipment

Order matters. Design the bar workflow for your projected covers and menu before you buy equipment. A station that looks beautiful but places the ice bin three steps from the pour point will slow service and increase spillage.

Map the guest journey, server routes, backbar access, glassware storage and cleaning flow. Scaled station plans prevent expensive mid-build changes.

## Realistic timeline

From concept to soft launch, a typical Bali bar setup takes 6–10 weeks excluding construction delay. Allow extra time for licence processing, supplier lead times and staff recruitment. Bring MyChef in 8–12 weeks before opening so layout, equipment and hiring decisions can be reviewed before they become expensive to change.

Plan a soft opening with friends, family and invited guests before marketing to the public. Use the feedback to refine recipes, service speed and payment workflows.

## Common mistakes: Common mistakes when opening a bar in Bali

- Signing a lease or ordering equipment before defining the concept, guest profile and price ladder.
- Treating licensing as an afterthought; SIUP-MB, TDUP and company structure take time and legal advice.
- Buying equipment before finalising bar layout, leading to workflow inefficiencies and rework.
- Underestimating working capital and the cash runway needed before revenue stabilises.
- Opening to the public without a soft launch to test recipes, service speed and payment workflows.
